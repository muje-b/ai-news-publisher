From BERT to Frontier Agents: Eight Years of Language-Model Progress, the Collapse of the Capability-Cost Curve, and the Rise of Task-Targeted Models

From the modest BERT model released in late 2018 to today’s frontier agents that can read code, write programs, and solve grade‑school math, the span of eight years shows a steady climb in what language models can do. The paper tracks this trajectory, noting how capability has risen while the price to run these models has fallen dramatically.

What You Need to Know

Between October 2018 and July 2026 the ability of models to resolve real coding issues improved by nearly six times per year, starting from late 2024. This growth outpaced earlier yearly gains and moved the field from simple language understanding to agents that can tackle multi‑step software tasks.

At the same time, the cost of running high‑performance models dropped sharply. OpenAI’s budget offering GPT‑5.6 Luna delivers flagship‑level performance for only one to six dollars per million tokens, a fraction of the price of its predecessors.

Top results are now split across specialized systems. Claude Opus 5 leads in frontend coding, Claude Fable 5 excels at repository‑level modifications, and GPT‑5.6 Sol dominates terminal‑based tasks. Each model is tuned for a narrow band of work rather than trying to be a universal solver.

Why It Matters

Lower inference costs mean that small teams and individual developers can access model quality that previously required large cloud budgets. This widens the pool of people who can prototype, debug, or generate code with AI assistance.

The move toward task‑targeted models shifts evaluation from a single “size‑wins” leaderboard to a fit‑for‑purpose approach. Organizations must now match model strengths to specific workflow steps, which can improve efficiency but also requires clearer task definitions.

Key Details

  • Coding issue resolution improved ~6×/year after late 2024.
  • GPT‑5.6 Luna costs $1‑6 per million tokens for flagship performance.
  • Claude Opus 5 tops frontend coding benchmarks.
  • Claude Fable 5 leads repository‑level coding tasks.
  • GPT‑5.6 Sol achieves the highest scores on terminal‑oriented challenges.
  • Qwen 2.5 solved 58/100 grade‑school math problems with basic prompting.

What's Next

Continued specialization will likely produce more models tuned for niches such as data analysis, scientific simulation, or domain‑specific language understanding. Monitoring the capability‑cost curve will remain essential for deciding when a larger, more general model is justified versus a smaller, task‑focused alternative.
