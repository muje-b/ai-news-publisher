World Series of Poker Returns to ESPN, Now With AI - Sportico.com

The World Series of Poker (WSOP) is coming back to ESPN, and this time the broadcast will feature artificial intelligence tools to help viewers follow the action. The move marks a continuation of the long‑standing partnership between the poker series and the sports network, while adding a new layer of technology aimed at making the game more accessible to both seasoned fans and newcomers.

What You Need to Know

ESPN will air the main WSOP events starting with the 2025 schedule, covering the opening day, the Main Event, and selected high‑stakes tournaments. The network has teamed up with an AI analytics provider that will generate real‑time statistics, hand‑strength estimates, and win‑probability graphics during each hand. These elements will appear as on‑screen overlays similar to those used in other sports broadcasts.

The AI system pulls data from the live feed, processes hole cards (when revealed), board texture, and betting patterns to calculate odds and suggest optimal play lines. Commentators will receive the same data through a production feed, allowing them to reference specific numbers when explaining a player’s decision. The technology is designed to complement, not replace, the human analysis that has long been a hallmark of poker commentary.

Viewers will see the AI graphics on ESPN’s linear channels as well as on the ESPN app and streaming platforms. The network says the feature will be optional for those who prefer a traditional broadcast, with a toggle to hide the overlays if desired. The rollout begins with the June 2025 WSOP schedule and will be evaluated throughout the series for potential expansion to other poker events.

Why It Matters

Integrating AI into a poker broadcast addresses a common barrier for new viewers: the difficulty of grasping the mathematical underpinnings of each hand. By displaying win probabilities and hand strength in real time, ESPN can demystify complex decisions without slowing the pace of the game. This approach mirrors innovations seen in other sports, where data visualizations have helped broaden audiences.

For the WSOP, the partnership also signals a commitment to modernizing its presentation while preserving the game’s core appeal. The AI tools can highlight subtle strategies that might otherwise go unnoticed, adding depth to the storytelling of each tournament. Advertisers may find value in the enriched broadcast, as clearer explanations can keep viewers engaged longer during long poker sessions.

Key Details

  • ESPN will broadcast the 2025 WSOP Main Event and selected preliminary tournaments.
  • AI analytics provider supplies real‑time win probability, hand strength, and betting‑trend graphics.
  • Overlays appear on linear TV, ESPN app, and streaming platforms; viewers can disable them.
  • Commentators receive the same AI data to enhance on‑air analysis.
  • The technology uses hole‑card information when cards are shown, combined with board texture and action.
  • Initial rollout runs through the summer 2025 WSOP series, with a review for future expansion.

What's Next

After the WSOP concludes, ESPN and the AI partner will assess viewer feedback and rating impact to determine whether the AI‑enhanced format becomes a permanent feature for poker coverage. If successful, similar data‑driven overlays could appear in other televised poker tours or even in live streaming of online tournaments, potentially shaping how the sport is presented across media platforms.
