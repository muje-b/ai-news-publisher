Europe’s AI labeling and transparency rules are now in effect

The European Union’s AI Act entered a new phase on August 2, when its transparency requirements became enforceable. Companies that provide generative models, chatbots, or tools that create synthetic media must now tell users when they are dealing with an artificial system. The rule applies to any service that interacts with EU residents, regardless of where the provider is based.

What You Need to Know

Any AI system that directly interacts with natural persons must display a clear notice that it is AI‑generated. This notice must be conspicuous, presented in the language of the user, and placed before or at the start of the interaction. It covers chatbots, voice assistants, and APIs that generate text or speech.

Providers of audio, video, or image synthesis must add a visible label (for example, “AI‑generated”) to the output and embed a machine‑readable tag in the file’s metadata. The label cannot be buried in terms of service or hidden behind a click‑through; it must be seen or heard by the person consuming the content.

Why It Matters

Transparency helps users avoid manipulation and make informed choices about the information they receive. By knowing when they are talking to a model or viewing synthetic media, people can better assess credibility and reduce the risk of being misled by deepfakes or automated persuasion.

For businesses, the rule creates a clear compliance baseline. Adjusting user interfaces, updating documentation, and training staff on labeling requirements can prevent costly fines and build trust with customers who value openness about AI use.

Key Details

  • The notice must be presented before or at the start of interaction, in a clear and unambiguous format.
  • For synthetic media, a visible label (e.g., “AI‑generated”) must accompany the content, plus a machine‑readable tag in metadata.
  • The rule covers all AI systems that have a direct interface with natural persons, including APIs used by third‑party services.
  • Exemptions apply only to systems used exclusively for internal research or testing without external exposure.
  • Enforcement began August 2
