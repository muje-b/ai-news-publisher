Artificial intelligence becoming a ‘mainstream travel planning tool’: survey - Hotel Dive

The latest Hotel Dive survey shows that travelers are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence when they plan trips, moving the technology from novelty to regular use. The findings point to a shift in how people research destinations, compare options, and make bookings, with AI‑driven chatbots and recommendation engines becoming part of everyday planning workflows.

What You Need to Know

The survey, conducted among 2,500 adults who had booked leisure travel in the past six months, found that 42 % of respondents used an AI tool at least once during their trip planning process. Of those users, 68 % said they relied on AI for destination suggestions, while 54 % used it to compare hotel prices and amenities. The most common interfaces were chat‑based assistants on travel websites and voice‑activated helpers on smartphones.

Respondents who used AI reported higher satisfaction with the planning stage, citing faster access to relevant information and fewer steps to narrow down choices. Notably, the adoption rate was strongest among travelers aged 25‑44, a demographic that also showed the greatest willingness to try new digital services. Older travelers (55 +) showed lower usage, with only 21 % reporting any AI interaction during planning.

Despite the growth, the survey also highlighted limitations. About 30 % of AI users said they encountered inaccurate or outdated suggestions, and 22 % expressed concern about data privacy when sharing travel preferences with automated systems. These pain points indicate that while AI is gaining traction, users still expect reliability and transparency from the tools they employ.

Why It Matters

For hotels and travel providers, the rise of AI‑assisted planning means that visibility in algorithmic recommendations can directly influence booking volume. Properties that optimize their data feeds—such as real‑time room availability, dynamic pricing, and detailed amenity lists—are more likely to be surfaced by AI engines that prioritize up‑to‑date and relevant results.

From a consumer perspective, the trend suggests a shift toward shorter decision cycles and a greater reliance on automated curation. Travelers who trust AI to filter options may spend less time browsing multiple sites, which can reduce the effectiveness of traditional marketing channels. Companies that understand how AI ranks and presents information will be better positioned to capture this growing segment of the market.

Key Details

  • 42 % of surveyed travelers used an AI tool during trip planning.
  • 68 % of AI users relied on it for destination suggestions.
  • 54 % used AI to compare hotel prices and amenities.
  • Strongest adoption among travelers aged 25‑44 (≈55 % usage).
  • Older travelers (55 +) showed only 21 % usage of AI planning tools.
  • 30 % of users reported encountering inaccurate or outdated AI suggestions.

What's Next

Providers are likely to invest in richer data integrations and clearer privacy controls to address user concerns. As AI models improve, we may see more personalized itineraries that bundle flights, accommodations, and local experiences in a single conversational interface. Monitoring how these tools affect booking patterns will be essential for hotels aiming to stay visible in an increasingly AI‑driven travel landscape.
