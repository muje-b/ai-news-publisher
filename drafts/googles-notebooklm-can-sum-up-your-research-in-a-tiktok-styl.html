Google’s NotebookLM is testing a new way to turn dense research into short, vertical video clips that look like they belong on TikTok. The feature takes the notes and source material you upload, runs them through its AI model, and spits out a 60‑second clip that mixes on‑screen text, a synthetic voice‑over, and simple graphics. It is currently being rolled out to users on the Google AI Ultra and Pro subscription tiers, with no announced date for broader availability.

What You Need to Know

The update builds on NotebookLM’s existing ability to summarize PDFs, web pages, and personal documents into plain‑text outlines. Instead of just delivering a bullet list, the new tool creates a video that follows a vertical 9:16 aspect ratio, optimized for mobile viewing. Each clip is limited to sixty seconds, which forces the AI to prioritize the most salient points from the uploaded sources.

Google demonstrated the feature with a sample notebook about Australia’s unsuccessful military campaign in the 1930s—commonly referred to as the Emu War. The AI pulled key facts from a handful of historical articles, generated a script, paired it with a neutral‑sounding voice, and added animated maps and caption bars that appear in sync with the narration. The result is a concise, watchable summary that can be shared directly from the app or exported as an MP4 file.

Why It Matters

For students, researchers, and professionals who already rely on NotebookLM to keep track of source material, the video option offers an alternative way to review information without rereading long PDFs. A sixty‑second recap can fit into a break between classes or during a commute, turning passive note‑storage into an active review habit.

From a productivity standpoint, the feature also reduces the friction of creating shareable micro‑content. Instead of filming a video yourself or hiring an editor, users can let the AI handle scriptwriting, voice synthesis, and basic visual layout, then tweak the output if needed. This could lower the barrier for teams that want to disseminate briefings or study guides in a format that feels native to short‑form platforms.

Key Details

  • Available to Google AI Ultra and Pro subscribers; rollout began in early June 2025.
  • Generates vertical video clips exactly 60 seconds long, 9:16 aspect ratio.
  • Input can include PDFs, Google Docs, web pages, or plain‑text notes already stored in NotebookLM.
  • AI produces a script, selects a synthetic voice, and adds timed captions and simple graphics.
  • Clips can be previewed, edited for text or timing, then exported as MP4 or shared via a link.
  • Google emphasizes that the model does not retain source files after video generation; data stays within the user’s workspace.

What's Next

Google says it will collect feedback from Ultra and Pro users to refine the voice quality, visual style, and length flexibility before considering a wider release. If the experiment proves useful, the company may extend the video summarization tool to the free tier or integrate it with other Workspace apps like Docs and Slides for automatic video abstracts.
